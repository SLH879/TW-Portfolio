# Multi-Platform Survey Administration and Data Review: Research Support Case Study
**Document ID:** Case-RESEARCH  -001 | **Version:** 1.0 | **Last Updated:** September 2026 | **Audience:** Research Teams | **Status:** Draft | **Published Date:** September 2026

## Overview

This case study documents survey and research work I previously completed for an independent graduate-level research project in mid to late 2024. Survey responses were collected from June 26–29, 2024, and the larger graduate-level research project was completed later that year. 

The research examined how social media use impacted consumer behaviour, identity formation, and mental well-being among Chinese young adults between ages 18 to 24. 

> My role in the project included developing and deploying an online questionnaire, translating the questionnaire between English and Simplified Chinese, administering it across two survey platforms, supporting participant recruitment and sampling, consolidating the results, and analyzing the collected data for trends, recurring, and abnormal findings. To protect the project and participants, this portfolio version does not reproduce the original questionnaire, institution, client, and response data. Any examples included herein are recreated and fictionalized.


## Project Context and Responsibilities

The project used a primarily quantitative review design structured around 17 comprehensive close and open ended online survey questions. Closed questions supported descriptive analysis, while open questions provided additional qualitative context.

The target population was Chinese young adults between ages 18 to 24 who used social media.

The survey explored many broad areas:
- social media usage
- pressures to conform
- identity and self presentation
- impacts on personal well-being

The final survey was designed on SurveyMonkey; subsequently deployed and administered on:
- Google Forms
- WJX.cn

## 1. Questionnaire Development

The online survey/questionnaire was initially developed on SurveyMonkey, before being transferred and administered on Google Forms and WJX.cn.

The questionnaire was developed with ease of access and thoroughness in mind. Questionnaires serve as a cheap and convenient method to collect data and gather insights. It is time-efficient, easy to administer, accessible across most devices with internet access. Google, in particular, is linked to other Google workspace applications, allowing for easy integration and transfer of data from Forms to Sheets.

I developed 17 comprehensive questions which combined a mix of multiple choice, truth and false, and open-ended questions.
- The multiple choice and truth/false questions were considered close-ended with preset response options and participants chose those that matched closest to how they felt. This made responses easier to compare.
- The short answer questions were considered open ended. This allowed participants to provide additional personalized context for their answers, allowing them to share unique narratives, experiences, and thoughts on how social media has impacted their consumption behaviour, identity formation, and mental well-being. 

The questionnaire was also developed to include a participant information and consent statement page, explaining: 
- The hypotheses, purpose, scope, data collection, information distribution, intended use of responses, and confidentiality measures. 
- Participants were required to acknowledge the statement before proceeding.


## 2. Privacy, Translation, and Platform Setup

To uphold privacy and participant confidentiality, participants were given a consent statement at the beginning of the online questionnaire specifying the nature, scope, and implications of the study. The statement aimed to be transparent; providing concrete information on the purpose, what the study covered and aimed to understand, and how the results would be collected, analyzed, and used in the study. Furthermore, the survey minimized the collection of identifying information such as last names, addresses, schools or workplace information. Identities were known during recruitment and consent phases, but access to survey questions were restricted to me and the client.

As the questionnaire was also administered on the Chinese surveying site, WJX.cn, Google Translate was used to provide an initial Simplified Chinese translation of the consent statement, questions and answers. English and Simplified Chinese were presented simultaneously so participants could conduct own translations and compare the translated wording to the source text. Questions and answers were also tweaked to reduce potential misinterpretations of the questions, as well as ensure concepts, slangs, figures of speech, and nuances in question structure were adequately communicated to Chinese participants. The survey also contained a consent statement at the top signifying the use of Google Translate for translation.

The platform have been selected because they were lightweight, easy to use, and accessible. Participants are able to access either platform through their phones, tablets, and computers. The platforms were identified through prior research, confirming accessibility across different electronic devices and to audiences across North America and China. 

    - Before launch, I completed a few test submissions to confirm that both versions can be opened across different common devices, that the required questions and answers appeared correctly, and that the responses could be submitted and retrieved. 
    - Participants were also reminded to reach out if they experience technical difficulties in accessing, answering, and submitting. 


## 3. Participant Recruitment and Data Collection

This study adopted convenience sampling method followed by random selection from the eligible participant pool. To better understand the phenomenon of social media's impacts on Chinese young adults' consumer behaviour, identity formation, and mental well-being, participants were recruited across North America and China. The goal was to maximize reach and generalizability, while creating parameters to ensure the study stays within manageable range. 

Through the convenience sampling method, I was able to initially recruit a pool of 60 participants, all of whom have confirmed through private message that they met the eligibility criteria: 
- Chinese, ages 18 to 24, use social media (Red, TikTok, Douyin, Facebook, and Instagram) frequently. 
    - An online random selector tool was then used to select 50 participants from that pool. 

Data collection occurred within a 72 hours window. 
- Participants were notified through the consent statement on the first page that they have 72 hours to complete the questionnaire, beginning on June 26, 2024 at 11:59PM BST (British Summer Time) and ending on June 29, 2024 at 11:59PM BST. 
- Participants were allowed to leave and resume as they wish, allowing them plenty of time and space to complete the survey in ways they saw fit. 


## 4. Response Consolidation and Review

Google Forms connects to Google Sheets and other parts of the workspace ecosystem, allowing for easy transfer of collected answers for analysis. Similarly, WJX.cn has a built-in dashboard that lists all raw data, as well as a summary section that generates graphs, charts, and tables with summaries, trends, and outliers.

After importing and consolidating the responses from Google Forms and WJX.cn in Google Sheets, I reviewed the closed-ended results for trends and unusual response patterns. I also coded the open-ended responses into common, quantifiable categories and conducted a basic thematic content analysis to identify and report recurring themes.

The analysis was exploratory and intended to support the larger research argument rather than establish findings representative of the full target population.


## Limitations

The methodology employed gave rise to a few identifiable limitations. Limitations include self-selection bias, accuracy, and survey fraud. 

### Self-Selection Bias
- My questionnaire may not have reached a broad enough young adult Chinese audience, such as those who differed in location, social media behaviour, or interest in the topic.
- Due to potential narrow reach, I was not able to control and reduce homogeneity, resulting in a potentially skewed and homogenous participation that may not be accurately representative of the broader Chinese young adult population. 
### Measurement and Response Bias
- Accuracy was hard to control because of varying interpretations of questions and answers
- There might have been translation and interpretation differences, resulting in varying understanding of the questions and answers presented
- Social desirability was hard to control, as participants might have answered in patterns that felt socially acceptable and normal.
- Varying states of mind and energy levels could lead to under and over-reporting. 
    * An example was the finding that most participants felt social media had no influence over their spending habits.
### Duplicate Response Risk
- The absence of direct personal identifiable indicators such as phone numbers, emails, and full names while deployed across two platforms, duplicate submissions could not be ruled out conclusively.
- This could have resulted in multiple submissions from the same person.
### Translation Inconsistency
- Translation work was not independently reviewed by a professional translator, differences in meanings could have remained and changed interpretations of questions and answers. For future multilingual survey work, I would use bilingual review, back-translation, and pilot testing before deployment.


## Lessons Learned

This research project has shown me the intricacies of conducting a survey-based research project combining quantitative and qualitative response data. There are many things to look out for throughout:
- Duplicate responses control - there is a need to implement unique identifiers for responses such as single participation codes to be used at the beginning of the questionnaire, and platform based participation limits (1 per session/device/browser).
- Translation accuracy - Google translation output may result in inconsistencies, so a professional translation service is needed to review and validate.
- Recruitment method limits generalizability - convenience sampling on social media and randomly selecting 50 did not create a population-level sample.
- Privacy and duplicate responses planning - aims to reduce collection of personal information made detecting duplicate submissions difficult.
